The psychiatrist told me today that I have been depressed for nearly a decade, or perhaps more.

He typed few words on his laptop

Wrote few medicines

Gave me an SOS helpline number

And handed over me the slip.

"Go to the counter, and make the payment"

"2000 rs only sir"

"You have been depressed for nearly a decade"

A decade.

MDD, panic attacks. Written in big bold letters which I saw from my peripheral vision. I did not want to see the slip, the diagnosis, or the paper.

I wanted to breathe. Instead.

The paper felt raw, even though my thumbs were numb.

I took a sip of water gulped it down and looked for the pharmacy store.

The legs were shaking. And so were the hands, and palms, and sweat and breath and ... everything. everything that there was in that room.

What do you do with this information that neural pathways in your brain have been permanently altered since a decade?

The tiny little neurons do not move as they should.

The hands cold a decade ago, were not you making excuses but panic attacks instead.

The breath really did not come, you were not making that shit up.

Should I sit down and make a list.

A validation list for everything that happened. So that I feel better.

Or should I bring the stars down and ask them, for justice, or mercy atleast?

The gods are kind they say.

Are they? I wonder as I look outside and try to process all this on a random afternoon while sitting in a coffee smelled hospital corridor all alone thinking about the last time the flowers tried to butcher the roots.

The roots survived. Flowers didn't.

Thank God, you say. Thank God? Really?

"How long will the meds go?"

It's a lifestyle disease now Ayush, it will stay.

"How long will the meds go?"

Think of it like diabetes or thyroid.

"How long will the meds go?"

This is the new normal. I can stop them if u insist but that won't be good.

"How long will the meds go?"

Very long.

Very long, he says.

There is no window, there is nowhere to look outside too. You search for an outlet. A vomit bin. A ladder to escape from. A dungeon. An outward prison box. There is nowhere to go.

The coffee now fills the room and has started sticking to your skin. You try to peel it off, hair by hair trying to establish the equilibrium among you and grief and doc and coffee and paper and medicines. You fail miserably.

Call the therapist. Or the ambulance. Or gods. Or maa. Or bhaiya. Or friends. Idk. Just call someone to take me out of this building. Call. Right now.

Breathe in.

Name five things you can see.

Name four things you can hear.

Name three things you can touch.

Name two things you can smell.

Name one thing you can taste.

Breathe out. Ayush. Breathe out.

A decade.

What to do with it now? Why now? You ask. Like a child sitting in a crib. All alone. Crying. In that corridor. All alone. To no one, you ask.
